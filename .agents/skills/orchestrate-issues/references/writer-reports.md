# Writer reports and requests

Messages between the coordinator and its writers are hints: they can queue, arrive late, be cut short or be lost. GitHub (issues, PR heads, review comments, CI) and each writer's own transcript are the authorities, so every decision rests on them, not on a message alone.

## The wake routine

Run it at every periodic check-in, on the first wake after the coordinator itself restarted or resumed from a summary, and before acting on any writer's report or request. A request a writer sent while the coordinator was not running is found only this way.

1. Snapshot GitHub and plan with an observation of every writer the record holds. The plan's `sweep` says which transcripts to read.
2. Read the end of the transcript of every writer under `sweep.read`, which lists every writer the record holds whose turn has ended, whatever its ticket's state, where the [writing routes](../../references/runner-writing.md) say its route keeps it (a detached CLI writer's is `out.jsonl` in its launch directory). Writers under `sweep.notRead` are running and are not read: a running writer cannot be waiting on the coordinator. Record each request found, as below.
3. Act on each ticket's action, reading every open PR's checks and merge state from its entry, and on every pending request in the record (`answer-request`) and every writer a previous coordinator briefed (`update-contact`). Settle every entry in the plan's `filed` list as [tickets filed during the run](#tickets-filed-during-the-run) describes.
4. Settle every `settle-delivery`: a brief its writer has not acknowledged within one check-in interval is a failed delivery. Confirm that the record's coordinator id is this session's own id. When the writer's turn has ended, read its transcript and record `acknowledged` if it shows one; a running writer's transcript is not read. Otherwise resend with `deliver --retry`, send that copy, and record `delivered`.
5. Raise every entry under `sweep.deadLaunches`, which checks every open PR, with its writer: a reviewer plan whose launcher ran, with no journal and no process naming it, died before its journal existed, and its `.coordinator.err.log` says why. A launcher that died after its child started leaves a journal the merge gate refuses until the writer runs `recover-lock` on its worktree, which settles it, or `agent-handoff.mjs settle-launch <journal>` after an earlier recovery ([handoff](../../review-pr/HANDOFF.md)). When that reviewer had already posted its own report, the round counts it only after the writer records that comment on the settled journal with `record-report` ([coordinator-posted reports](../../review-pr/HANDOFF.md#coordinator-posted-reports)); it is never relaunched.
6. Dispatch the dependency-eligible tickets the plan admits, and act on its `checkIn` entry; after the coordinator's own restart or resume it reports `rearm`, and the coordinator re-arms the job as its next step names.
7. Record the outcome with `checked-in --outcome "<what was done>"`, or `--outcome "no change"` when nothing needed action.

## How writers reach the coordinator

Each route's way of messaging the coordinator is in the runner configuration (`reports` in `.claude/writer-defaults.json`, keyed by the coordinator's runner). The brief carries the recorded session identity and title beside the contact instruction. A title-addressed writer resolves the current title through its runner's session listing, stops on ambiguous or missing matches, and retains the id and host as identity cross-checks. Every answer and resume refreshes that instruction, including for an existing writer whose coordinator has not changed.

A writer sends every report, blocker and question through its configured route, then ends its turn. Native child writers use the parent task address supplied at launch after confirming its identity; that address is distinct from the desktop thread UUID and title. App writers outside that native tree and separate CLI processes retain transcript reporting where no messaging route has been verified. Cross-runner routes marked unsupported also use that fallback: end each decision point with the report as the turn's last message, and the check-in reads it from the transcript.

The writer's first act is an acknowledgement naming its assignment and the model, effort and permission mode it confirmed from its own metadata. Record it with `acknowledged --ticket N --model --effort --permission-mode --evidence "<where it was read>"`. It shows the brief arrived and its configured contact route reaches this coordinator; a confirmation that differs from the start plans as `stop-writer`.

## Read a writer's report in full

A note that a writer ended its turn, or a message relaying its report, may be a fixed-length excerpt: treat it as a pointer to the report, never the report itself. A report is complete only when it names the model, effort and permission mode the writer read itself, the PR, the last independently reviewed commit, the rounds used per reviewer route, the outstanding findings (or None), the verification, the state of each CI check on the final head, the tickets it filed (or None), and the writer's next action. When a note lacks any of these, read the end of that writer's transcript before deciding anything, and confirm the PR facts on GitHub. Store the full report with `result`; one naming another model, effort or mode than the start is kept and plans as `stop-writer`. A writer claimed before its brief asked for the filed tickets, or adopted with its work, reports without them, and `result` says to read them from GitHub instead.

## Tickets filed during the run

A writer's outstanding-work sweep, its filing routes, review-pr's deferral Tasks and the coordinator's own filings all create tickets during the run, and none of them lands in the parent's tree by itself. The coordinator places each one when it learns of it, from a writer's report or at a check-in, not at the end of the run.

1. Confirm it on GitHub. The report's `filed` field, `None` or a list of `{ "number", "reading" }` entries (`result` refuses prose), is a hint, so the plan lists a ticket it names as `confirm-filed` until the record holds it. Read the PR report's Notes for reviewers, or the ticket's provenance line naming the parent and the PR it came from, then record it with `filed --filed N --ticket <slice> --pr <P> --source "<where GitHub shows it>" --reading "<the writer's reading>"` (no `--ticket` or `--pr` for the coordinator's own filing). A reused existing issue is a filing like any other: record it with `filed`, its source saying it was reused, and triage it; its age is no ground for dismissal. Only a number GitHub shows is no ticket the writer filed or reused (a mistyped number, the writer's own slice or the parent) is recorded with `dismiss --filed N --ticket <slice> --evidence "<what GitHub shows>"` instead. Each follow-up a writer proposed without filing is decided too: file it through the [filing routes](../../references/runner-filing.md) and record it, or name it for the maintainer.
2. Read the ticket against the PR it came from. The writer's reading is a hint, never the decision. Record one outcome with `triage --filed N --outcome <kind>`:
   - `completes --slice S --reason`: the ticket names the same root cause as the slice's own defect, would replace or remove code the slice's PR adds (a special case, a guard, a suppressed error), or calls that change a workaround or an interim fix. It is always in scope. While S's PR is open, attach the ticket under the parent with the parent's milestone and send the PR back to its writer, as an answer or a resume, to make the proper fix there with a closing reference to the ticket; the planner then counts it delivered by S, and the review round count continues. It takes no blocker, since S's own PR delivers it. Once S's PR has merged, the ticket runs on its own: it goes under the parent with acceptance criteria that include removing the narrow fix, and is placed as `needed` is below, milestone and chain-tail blocker included. Until S is delivered the planner holds the ticket, for a new writer and for one continuing existing work alike, and a ticket whose S has left the tree plans `decision-needed`.
   - `needed --under U --reason`: the parent's goal or one of its acceptance criteria needs it, or it is a defect in behavior the run's own slices changed. Attach it under the parent, or under the container in the tree that owns that area, copy the parent's milestone to it, and make it `blocked_by` the tail of its file set's serial chain as [slicing](slicing.md) chooses it, so it cannot race a slice in flight. The next snapshot and plan treat it like any other sub-issue.
   - `separate --under U --reason`: it is work outside the parent's goal, and it still goes in a tree. Attach it under the open umbrella whose Scope covers it, copy that umbrella's milestone, and add the `Added on` line to its Scope as the [shared step](../../references/publishing.md#place-a-filed-ticket) does. When only a closed umbrella's Scope fits, reopen it with a comment naming the ticket and attach it there. Record `maintainer` instead only when no umbrella exists, with the question of where the ticket belongs.
   - `maintainer --question [--slice S]`: the ticket, or the proper fix it describes, changes agreed behavior or needs a major architectural commitment. Raise it with the maintainer as a question. With `--slice`, S's PR holds until the answer is recorded. When the maintainer accepts the narrow fix as an interim, record `interim --filed N --decision "<their words>" --evidence "<where>"`: S's PR may merge, and the ticket goes under the parent as completing that fix, so the parent cannot close without it. Since it then runs on its own after S's merge, place it as `needed` is, with the milestone and a `blocked_by` on its file set's chain tail; S is that tail or comes before it, since the ticket removes S's narrow fix.
3. Attach with the REST sub-issue call [slicing](slicing.md) uses, never re-parenting a ticket that already has a parent, a PR, a worktree or a writer. Copy the milestone as slicing does: read the number with `gh api repos/<tickets>/issues/<from> --jq .milestone.number` and set it with `gh api -X PATCH repos/<tickets>/issues/<N> -F milestone=<number>`. Read the parent link and the milestone back with `gh api repos/<tickets>/issues/<N>/parent --jq .number` and `gh api repos/<tickets>/issues/<N> --jq .milestone.number`. Project membership follows the parent link, so no project field is written.

The plan reports every recorded ticket without an outcome, or with a separate outcome that names no umbrella, as `triage-filed`, `close-issue` refuses while one remains, and the merge gate holds a slice's PR while a ticket that completes its fix is open and not closed by it, unless the record holds the maintainer's interim decision.

## Record each request and its answer

When a writer asks for a decision (another review round, a design question, a blocker), record it with `request --ticket N --text "<the request>" --source "<where it was read>"`, which prints its id. The request belongs to the writer that asked it: it closes when that writer is released, and a new writer asks its own. The plan shows the ticket as `answer-request` until the answer has been delivered. A request passed to the maintainer is recorded with `relay --ticket N --request <id> --to "the maintainer"` and stays open while they decide.

An answer is a message, so it is recorded around its sending. `answer --ticket N --request <id> --text "<the answer>"` records the intent and prints the message to send; send it exactly as printed, then record `answered --ticket N --request <id>`. A detached CLI writer's process ends with its turn, so its request stays open after that and is answered by resuming its session, the same way it is resumed to continue work it stopped on: write the printed message to a file and run `resume` with it as the prompt, as the [writing routes](../../references/runner-writing.md) describe, then record `answered`. The resumed process becomes the writer; its assignment and requests stay the same. Each copy quotes the question it answers, names the request and its delivery, and tells the writer to ignore a copy once one has arrived. If the coordinator stops between the two records, the answer may still be queued: when the writer's turn has ended and its transcript shows it, record `answered` with that evidence; otherwise wait, or resend with `answer --retry`, which prints the same answer with a new delivery id. Never record a new answer over an unconfirmed one.

The brief went with the writer's start, and a resend follows the same steps: `deliver --retry` prints the stored brief under a new delivery id, `delivered` confirms it, and every copy names the assignment so the writer acts on the first it receives. An existing writer adopted with its work already has its assignment and is not briefed again.

After a takeover, a writer a previous coordinator briefed still reports to that coordinator. Before any brief resend reaches it, `contact --ticket N` records the intent and prints a notice naming this coordinator's id and how to reach it; send it, then record `contacted`, with the same retry rule as an answer. A resent brief names this coordinator too but never settles the contact, since a writer that already has its assignment ignores the copy. An answer from this coordinator, and a resume of a writer whose turn ended, end with a contact line that holds even when the writer sets the rest aside as a copy it already has, so recording either delivered records the contact.

Writers do not post a separate protocol of comments for these exchanges; their normal review comments stay on the PR's review thread (the PR, or its internal ticket once the cutover switch is on).
