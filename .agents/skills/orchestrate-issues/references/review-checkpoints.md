# Review checkpoints

A writer's review reaches a formal checkpoint once round 3's accepted fixes are verified, and again after every later round's. Before round 3 there is none: a writer's ordinary review continues, and examining the root cause of each accepted defect belongs to every correction. The checkpoint is the coordinator's judgement on the whole PR, not on its round count. The helper is `node .agents/skills/orchestrate-issues/review-checkpoint.mjs <command>`; commands that change the run record take `--record`, `--coordinator` and `--expect-revision` like the run helper's.

## When a checkpoint is due

`evidence --record <run.json> --ticket N --pr P --diff-out <file>` reads the PR's review journals under `<main checkout>.review-jobs/pr-<P>` (found by their contents under whatever name the launcher was given, each with the plan that names it as its journal; a count-form round whose plan is not there has an unknown reviewer count and holds the checkpoint), the comments of the PR's review thread, and the PR's head and checks, and prints what the checkpoint decides on, with every reason it is not yet due. It reads the journals through the launcher's own round accounting (`reviewAccounting` and `launchEvidence` in `scripts/agent-handoff.mjs`, which lock recovery also uses), so the checkpoint and the launcher never count a round differently. It is due when every planned reviewer of the last complete round, round 3 or later, has a validated completion; every review launch has an `exited` row and every launcher has finished; every report is on the review thread with a later comment headed as its adjudication that names it; no later round has started; CI has started on the head with no failed check; and the decisions published on the review thread account for every round after the third. A round's planned reviewers are the ones its first launch named, or the count its step declared, fixed before any reviewer ran, and it is complete once each has a completion here; a later launch or plan declaring other reviewers or another count is an audit violation, not a different round. A launch with a delegated execution service has ended only once its `exited` row records `executionDrained: true`. Once a round is complete, a reviewer of it launched again on another head is a new round, whatever attempt or plan it claims. A round after the third needs, as the decision in force at its first launch, a trusted extension decided after the round before it: a later question, repair or stop withdraws an earlier extension, in the record's order. A round whose reviewers are unnamed is complete when the completions here number the reviewers its step declared: a count a journal carries does not stand in for another reviewer's journal and report. A report the coordinator posted for a reviewer counts by its `report-posted` row. A launch that reserved a slot and recorded neither its start nor its exit may still run. Journals that ran on another machine are not here: every round the review thread's round-state comments show without a journal blocks the checkpoint until it is read where it ran.

## Read and assess

Read the PR's design, its review thread's round-state comments, every report and adjudication, and the unreviewed diff that `--diff-out` wrote. Then write the assessment, a JSON file:

- `mechanisms`: which accepted findings share an underlying mechanism, and whether the corrections prevent that class of failure or patch its instances.
- `regressions`: which defects the corrections themselves introduced, and what that says about stability or verification.
- `repairOptions`: whether a simpler or broader repair would improve the system, with the experiment or code evidence behind that.
- `residualRisk`, `coverage`, `nextRoundValue`: the concrete risks that remain, the verification that covers them, and what another independent round is expected to establish.
- `rationale`, `next`: the decision's reasoning and the next action.
- `agreedBehavior` (`preserved` or `changed`) and `architecture` (`unchanged` or `major-change`).
- `findings`: every finding of every round, `{ "comment": <report id>, "finding": "<short>", "disposition": "accepted|rejected|covered|deferred|open" }`, and `{ "comment": <id>, "disposition": "none" }` for a report with none. Known unresolved defects and material verification gaps stay `open`; they are not hypothetical future edge cases.

The helper refuses a missing field, a report the findings omit, and an open finding under `finish` or `accept-unreviewed`. It cannot tell sound reasoning from filled-in words; the published decision is where that is read and judged.

## Decide

`decide --record --coordinator --expect-revision --ticket N --pr P --kind <kind> --assessment <file> --out <body.md>` takes the evidence again, refuses a decision whose conditions fail, records it and writes the comment to post. The kinds:

| Kind | When | What it records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `finish` | The review gates pass. | Ordinary review-pr readiness decides the PR; the decision grants nothing beyond it. |
| `repair` | Findings share a mechanism that a broader or simpler repair addresses while keeping the parent issue's agreed behavior. | `scope` (what widens, across components if needed) and `verificationChange`. The round count stays; once the repair is verified, decide again on the changed risk. |
| `extend` | Another round is expected to establish something concrete. | `limit` (above the completed round, at most ten) and `limitReason`. The writer's next launcher plan sets `reviewRoundLimit` to it and `extendedReviewAuthorization` to the invocation the decision quotes, verbatim. |
| `accept-unreviewed` | Root causes were addressed and further review has diminishing benefit. | See below. |
| `maintainer` | A material concern, a change to the agreed behavior or a major architectural commitment. | `question`. The ticket waits for the answer; independent tickets continue. It may be recorded while other conditions fail, and then names each one instead of the passed gates. |

When one mechanism keeps producing findings round after round, another round reviewing its instances is not converging: choose a `repair` that removes the mechanism's second implementation or the mechanism itself, then a bounded round on the repair. Do this even when the repair widens the PR, provided the agreed behavior holds. Prefer a simplification that drops a claim when that mechanism's failure mode is false assurance, such as a recovery it cannot prove; an honest limitation beats a narrower claim. Accept unreviewed corrections only once the defect classes have stopped recurring and what remains unreviewed is small and verified. The check's recorded scenarios include both shapes from this parent issue's own run.

A changed agreed behavior or a major architectural change is refused under every kind but `maintainer`. Round ten admits no extension; reaching it never grants acceptance, which keeps its own conditions. A decision's round never goes below an earlier decision's, and nothing resets the count: a repair, a scope change, a new journal, a takeover or another account, model or machine.

## Accepted unreviewed corrections

Only the coordinator accepts final unreviewed corrections, and only under the invocation it quotes. The helper refuses it unless something after the last independently reviewed commit is unreviewed and descends from it; every finding is resolved; every CI check on the head has passed (none pending); `verification` lists what passed on the head; and `--inspected` gives the unreviewed diff's SHA-256 from the evidence, which binds the decision to the exact range read. Every decision is refused when the coordinator's own session ever wrote for the ticket: its writer, a released one, or a pending start. The range is the branch's first-parent commits after the reviewed head. A two-parent merge whose other parent is on origin/main appears as its remerge diff, since the base's commits were reviewed in their own PRs; any other merge, including one of three or more parents, for which Git gives no remerge diff, appears with every commit it brings in and its whole change against its first parent.

The decision applies to that head only. `acceptance-check --record <run.json> --pr P` says whether the PR's latest trusted acceptance applies to its current head: the run record must have published that comment with that content, and a new push, or a later decision in the record's order, ends it, and a new checkpoint decides the new head. An acceptance is not ordinary readiness and not an independent review. With an acceptance that applies, the coordinator marks the PR ready itself and says so in a comment naming the decision; the writer does not, and the writer's own handoff stays an incomplete draft. The coordinator then merges it only through the merge gate in [integration](integration.md).

## Publish, read back and restore

Post the body on the PR's [review thread](../../references/publishing.md#the-review-thread) with the command `decide` prints: `gh pr comment P --body-file <body.md>` while the run's tickets are in its code repository, and `gh issue comment N -R <tickets> --body-file <body.md>` on the ticket once the record names a tickets repository apart, when nothing is posted on the public PR. Read the comment back into a file through the paginated API, then record `published --ticket N --id <decision> --comment <id> --read-back <file>`, which refuses a read-back that differs from the decision. Until it is published, no other decision for the ticket is recorded, and the decision grants nothing, even after a takeover: an unpublished acceptance never applies and an unpublished extension authorizes no round. It does withdraw what came before it, from when it was made, so an interrupted hold is already in effect; a coordinator holding the record settles an interrupted one by finding the comment and recording `published` with its read-back, or closes one never posted with `abandon --id --evidence`. Send the writer the decision's text as the answer to its request.

The review thread is the decision's authority: the PR, or the ticket once tickets live apart. Each comment ends with a machine-readable block holding the decision: its kind, round, last reviewed commit, head, unreviewed range and diff hash, limit and authority, CI, reports, any unmet conditions, and the assessment. `restore --pr P` with `--trust <coordinator session>` (a coordinator taking over names the coordinators the parent issue's status lists) reads every decision from the review thread alone, with its exact head and range; without `--record`, `--repo` and the cutover switch say where that thread is, and `--ticket N` names it once the switch is on. Writers and coordinators post under one GitHub account, so neither a comment's author, nor the name in a block, nor the order of comments proves who decided or what withdrew what.

With a run record, the record is the authority on order. The decisions in effect are the record's own decisions for the PR, published or recorded but not yet read back, in the order of the record's own list, never re-sorted by a clock; they include those of coordinators it took over from. Each takes effect when the record made it; an extension authorizes a round only if it was published before that round's first launch. Alongside them is any decision of a predecessor the record names for the ticket and does not hold.

A recorded decision is verified only when its recorded comment still carries exactly its content, and only a verified acceptance ever applies. A later decision in effect replaces an earlier one, verified or not. Nothing done to a comment changes what is in effect: editing a block, damaging or removing it, deleting the comment, or copying a recorded id into another comment.

Without a record, the decisions in effect are the trusted blocks in comment order. They serve round accounting only, since none is verified.

A predecessor's decision is trusted but unverified, and counts for round accounting and nothing more. Trust is per ticket: a predecessor named for one ticket is not trusted on another, and no session that wrote for a ticket is ever trusted on it. A writer held by its process is known by its session, which the record keeps once the writer's stream has been read. When neither the record nor a stream readable on this machine names that session, the ticket's writers are unknown, and every decision, trust and merge that depends on them is refused until `issue-run.mjs writer-session --ticket N --writer <assignment or process id> --session <id> --evidence <where it was read>` records it. Every other block is reported with why it is untrusted, and malformed blocks are reported, never thrown.

A coordinator resuming with none of its predecessor's records names each predecessor for the ticket with `trust-predecessor --record --coordinator --expect-revision --ticket N --session <predecessor> --source "<where the parent issue names it>"`, from the parent issue's status comment; its own session and any session that wrote for the ticket are refused. The predecessor's decisions then account for the rounds they authorized, but none is verified. To finish the ticket, the successor reads the PR and decides and publishes its own checkpoint, including any acceptance, on the current head.

The PR body's Notes for reviewers (the latest PR report's, once the cutover switch is on) distinguishes the code independently reviewed through the last reviewed commit, any accepted unreviewed range with its decision, and the work still unresolved.
